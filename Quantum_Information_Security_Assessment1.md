## 1. Choosing Your Quantum Concept 

You are required to select a quantum principle or concept relevant to the module and prepare a short presentation demonstrating your understanding of it. 

Examples include superposition, measurement, entanglement, the no-cloning theorem and quantum key distribution (QKD). You may also choose an appropriate concept that has not yet been covered, or is not directly covered, in the module, such as quantum teleportation, Grover's algorithm, Shor's algorithm or quantum error correction. 

If you choose a broad topic, you should focus on a specific aspect that can be meaningfully explained within three minutes. 

Where appropriate to your chosen concept, you should consider: 

- its benefits and limitations; 

- how the principle can be demonstrated through a quantum circuit or quantum programming implementation; and 

- an example of a practical or potential use case. 

You are not required to write quantum code from scratch. You may use examples, code, circuits or simulation results from the practical labs provided as part of this module. What is being assessed is your ability to explain what the implementation demonstrates and how it relates to your chosen quantum concept, rather than your ability to program independently.

### 7¡.Three‗Minute.Recorded.Explanation 

You are required to submit a recorded presentation of **no more than three minutes** explaining your chosen concept in your own words. 

Imagine that you are explaining the concept to a technically literate cybersecurity professional who has little or no previous knowledge of quantum computing. 

Three minutes is deliberately short. You are not expected to explain everything about your chosen topic. Part of the assessment is your ability to identify the most important aspects and communicate them clearly and concisely. 

Your explanation should address the following: 

- What is it? 

   - Explain the essential idea clearly and accurately. 

- How does it work? 

   - Use one well-chosen example, diagram, quantum circuit, analogy or simple scenario to demonstrate how the concept works. 

   - Where appropriate, make a connection to a quantum circuit or quantum programming implementation. You may use or capture an example from the practical labs provided in this module. You are not required to develop the code yourself, but you should be able to explain what the implementation demonstrates. 

- What are its benefits and limitations? 

   - Where appropriate, identify an important benefit and limitation of the principle or its application. 

   - Where can it be used and why does it matter? 

   - Provide an example of a use case or potential application. Where relevant, relate this to quantum computing, information security or cybersecurity. 

You do not need to give equal time to each of these elements. Focus on those that are most meaningful for your chosen concept. 

You may use a small number of slides or a single visual to support your explanation. These should support rather than replace your spoken explanation. 

The emphasis is on your understanding and your ability to communicate the concept, not on sophisticated slides, video editing or production quality.

#### **_Challenge Question *_** 

You are required to formulate one question that another student should be able to answer after watching your presentation, and provide your own answer to that question. 

Your question should test understanding rather than simple recall. For example, it could: 

- ask what would happen if something changed; 

- require the concept to be applied to a new situation; 

- explore an important consequence or limitation; or 

- challenge a common misunderstanding. 

The purpose of the Concept Artefact is not to produce a miniature essay. Focus on selecting and communicating meaningful information concisely. 

## 3. Authentication Interview

If selected, you will be asked a small number of questions directly related to your own submission. The interview is intended to authenticate your understanding of the work you have submitted. It is not an additional examination of material outside your chosen topic. 

If you are invited to an authentication interview, attendance is compulsory. ## 4. Use of Generative AI - Scale 2: AI Planning 

This assessment is designated Scale 2 – AI Planning. 

You may use generative AI during the planning stage of your assessment. For example, you may use AI to: 

- explore possible topics; 

- identify aspects of a topic worth investigating; 

- brainstorm ideas or examples; 

- help develop an initial outline; or 

- support initial research and identify areas that you should investigate further. 

You should independently verify information obtained through generative AI using appropriate academic or technical sources. 

AI should not be used to generate or draft your presentation script, final presentation, Concept Artefact, or Challenge Question and answer. These elements are intended to demonstrate your own understanding and communication of the topic. 

You are required to include a brief declaration stating whether you used generative AI and, if so, how you used it during the planning process. 

Use of AI within these permitted boundaries will not be penalised.
